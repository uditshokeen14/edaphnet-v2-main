Soil laboratory report verification · research prototype

EdaphNet

Soil test reports are sometimes wrong: a sample is swapped with another, or a number is mistyped. EdaphNet asks whether a cheap, independent look at the same soil (its colour, as a phone camera sees it) can tell a lab which reports to re-test and what went wrong.

19,746 real soil samples (KSSL) · 3,473 (ICRAF-ISRIC) Pre-registered pass/fail rules Every number traced to a run record

Where the project stands

Works

  • Finding swapped samples, which simple checks cannot see.
  • Explaining mistakes: right error type 97–100% vs 24–29% for rule-based repair (spectrometer witness).
  • Reliable false-alarm control once calibration uses thousands of distinct verified reports.

Did not pass

  • With a phone-grade colour witness, re-testing finds about as many mistakes as the best existing method (pre-registered claim failed).
  • Single wrong values are found better by simple checks.

Next

  • Combine EdaphNet (swaps) with a value checker (draft plan).
  • Real phone photos and real lab errors from Indian soils.
  • Confirmation on the LUCAS dataset.
